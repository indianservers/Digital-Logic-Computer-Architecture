Operating Principle & Engineering Context

Star trackers and inertial sensors estimate satellite orientation. Feedback control commands reaction wheels or other actuators to reduce attitude error. The simulation integrates a one-axis rigid body with bounded PD torque and reaction-wheel angular momentum; it is a reduced teaching model.

Estimate satellite orientation and coordinate actuators to follow a pointing target. The Satellite Attitude-Control Computer signal path begins with star tracker / gyroscopes and leads to orientation feedback. Between these endpoints, attitude estimation, pointing target, control law, reaction wheels / actuators connect the physical system to its digital processing and output.

◈ Working Principle — Step by Step

  1. Acquire star-tracker and gyro measurements.

  2. Combine absolute orientation with angular-rate data.

  3. Calculate error relative to the pointing objective.

  4. A bounded proportional-derivative law requests torque.

  5. Wheel momentum changes produce opposite spacecraft torque.

  6. Measure the new orientation and repeat.

◈ Safety & Reliability

• Wheel torque and momentum are bounded.

• Real spacecraft require three-axis estimation and momentum unloading.

• Sensor faults invalidate the teaching attitude output.

◈ Key Takeaways
