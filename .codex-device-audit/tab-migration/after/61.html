Operating Principle & Engineering Context

A primary flight display combines air-data, inertial attitude and navigation information into an instrument presentation. The horizon rotates with bank; speed, altitude and heading retain their own units and validity.

Turn attitude, altitude and airspeed data into a synchronized flight-instrument view. The Primary Flight Display signal path begins with attitude / air-data sensors and leads to flight display. Between these endpoints, aircraft data bus, validity checks, display computation, graphics pipeline connect the physical system to its digital processing and output.

◈Sensors & Data Inputs

  • AHRS — attitude, rate and heading
  • Air Data Computer — airspeed, altitude and vertical speed
  • GPS / FMS — position, track and navigation guidance
  • Avionics buses — validated source data and status

◈How It Works — 8 Steps

  1. Sensors provide attitude, speed, altitude and heading.

  2. The interface receives avionics messages.

  3. Check data validity and calculate display values.

  4. Draw instrument tapes and the artificial horizon.

  5. Present synchronized flight parameters.

  6. Add heading and route guidance.

  7. The pilot interprets the instrument indication.

  8. Refresh as measurements change.

◈Real-World Applications

  • Aircraft instrument displays
  • Flight training
  • Avionics maintenance education

◈Safety / Limitations

  • Failed sources require visible flags.
  • Attitude and air-data sources are distinct.
  • No real flight guidance is provided.

◈Key Takeaways

  • Air data, attitude and navigation share one display.
  • Pitch and bank move the artificial horizon.
  • Speed and altitude keep separate scales and units.
  • Heading helps the pilot interpret flight direction.
  • Invalid source data must be visibly flagged.
