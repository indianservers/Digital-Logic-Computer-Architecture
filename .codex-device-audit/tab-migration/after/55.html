Operating Principle & Engineering Context

A controlled motor rotates balanced samples. Relative centrifugal force is RCF = 1.118 × 10⁻⁵ × radius(cm) × RPM². Sedimentation depends on particle density, size, medium and time; the displayed separation fraction is an illustrative kinetic model.

◈How It Works — 7 Steps

  1. Place matched masses in opposite rotor positions.

  2. The interlock confirms the lid is securely locked.

  3. Select speed, duration and the correct rotor radius.

  4. Closed-loop speed control compares tachometer feedback with the target.

  5. Density and sedimentation characteristics determine sample migration.

  6. Controlled braking reduces rotor speed before access is allowed.

  7. The lid releases only after the rotor has stopped.

◈Safety / Limitations

  • Lid interlock inhibits rotation when open.
  • Unbalanced loads trigger a vibration alarm.
  • Rotor speed must remain within its rated limit.
  • Access waits for the rotor to stop.

◈Key Takeaways

  • Rotation separates components according to density.
  • RCF increases with rotor radius and the square of RPM.
  • Balanced tubes reduce vibration and protect the rotor.
  • The lid remains locked until rotation stops.
  • Separation also depends on sample, medium and time.
