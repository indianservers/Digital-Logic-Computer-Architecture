Operating Principle & Engineering Context

A microbolometer array responds to incident long-wave infrared radiation. Readout electronics, digitization and nonuniformity correction form a thermal image. The synthetic field illustrates emissivity and reflected ambient radiation using a fourth-power radiance approximation, rather than calibrated thermography.

Map infrared radiation onto a detector array and convert it into a temperature-related image. The Thermal Imaging Camera signal path begins with infrared radiation and leads to thermal image. Between these endpoints, ir optics, detector array, readout / adc, correction / image processing connect the physical system to its digital processing and output.

◈ Working Principle — Step by Step

  1. Temperature drives emitted infrared radiation.

  2. The lens focuses emitted infrared radiation.

  3. Microbolometer pixels change resistance as they warm.

  4. A readout IC scans the detector array.

  5. ADC and nonuniformity correction reduce pixel offsets.

  6. Convert the synthetic field into a display range.

  7. Color-map intensity and present the image.

  8. Continuously acquire and process new thermal frames.

◈ Advantages, Limitations & Safety

• Low emissivity makes reflections significant.

• The image is synthetic and not a calibrated temperature measurement.

• Thermal imaging differs from reflected near-infrared night imaging.
