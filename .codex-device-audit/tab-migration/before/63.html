◈INS in Aircraft Navigation

◈System Architecture / Circuit

◈How It Works — 7 Steps

  1. Gyros sense rotation and accelerometers sense specific force.

  2. Update orientation from angular-rate measurements.

  3. Rotate measurements into the navigation frame.

  4. Gravity-compensated acceleration changes velocity.

  5. Velocity updates estimated position.

  6. External measurements can constrain drift.

  7. Report attitude, velocity, position and status.

◈Drift & Error Behavior

Constant accelerometer bias integrates twice. GNSS aiding attenuates bias in this educational model.

◈Safety / Limitations

  • Bias accumulates through integration.
  • Alignment is required before navigation.
  • The one-dimensional teaching model omits full earth-frame navigation.

◈Key Takeaways

Gyroscopes propagate attitude while accelerometers measure specific force. After transforming to a navigation frame and compensating gravity, integration yields velocity and position. Bias integrates into drift; optional aiding constrains the simplified one-dimensional estimate.
