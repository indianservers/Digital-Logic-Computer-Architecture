◈ Real Hardware / Device Overview

A two-wheeled robot is an inverted pendulum. An IMU estimates pitch and angular rate; a PID controller commands wheel torque to oppose falling. Motor drivers apply signed demand and wheel motion accelerates the base beneath the body. This reduced linear model shows saturation and the loss of balance outside its small-angle region.

◈ Working Principle (Step by Step)

  1. The body tilts away from its upright target.

  2. Accelerometer and gyro measurements estimate pitch and rate.

  3. The MCU updates its filtered state estimate.

  4. Angle error, accumulated error and rate determine wheel demand.

  5. Motor commands saturate at the teaching limit.

  6. Signed H-bridge output accelerates the wheels.

  7. Wheel motion shifts the base under the body.

  8. The controller continuously updates its feedback loop.

◈ Key Takeaways

✓ An IMU combines acceleration and angular-rate sensing

✓ Angle estimation provides real-time tilt feedback

✓ PID uses proportional, integral and derivative terms

✓ Motor drivers convert control demand into wheel torque

✓ Wheel motion moves the base beneath the body

✓ Fast repeated feedback is essential for balance

✓ Linearized dynamics apply near upright.

✓ Large tilt or saturation can lose balance.

✓ Hardware is represented by a local model.
