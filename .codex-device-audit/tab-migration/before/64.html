◈System Architecture / Circuit

◈How It Works — 6 Steps

  1. Satellites broadcast timed navigation signals.

  2. The antenna and RF front end acquire signals.

  3. Tracking loops estimate code phase and pseudorange.

  4. Decode satellite orbit and clock information.

  5. Estimate receiver position and clock offset.

  6. Publish position, velocity, time and integrity information.

  • Aircraft navigation

  • Route displays
  • Satellite geometry education

◈Safety / Limitations

  • Insufficient satellites prevent an unconstrained 3D fix.
  • Multipath and poor geometry degrade accuracy.
  • This model illustrates uncertainty, not a real GNSS solver.

◈Key Takeaways

A receiver correlates GNSS signals to obtain pseudoranges. A position solution estimates three spatial coordinates and receiver-clock offset. At least four suitable satellite observations are needed for an unconstrained three-dimensional solution; geometry and errors affect accuracy.

Inputs: Transmit timing signals. Output: Display navigation.
