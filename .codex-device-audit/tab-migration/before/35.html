Build This Device — System Architecture
  1. 1. Collimator

    Geometric apertures constrain the directions reaching or leaving the imaging system. In gamma imaging, they trade sensitivity for spatial resolution.

  2. 2. Scintillation crystal

    Incident radiation deposits energy in a scintillator, which emits optical photons for subsequent detection.

  3. 3. Photomultiplier tubes

    Incoming light produces an electrical response. Optical conversion or gain occurs before the analog signal is conditioned and sampled.

  4. 4. Event-position electronics

    Position sensing reports mechanical motion to the controller. Comparing desired and observed position produces the servo error.

  5. 5. Rotating gantry

    Rotating gantry connects to the acquisition or control chain. A collimator selects gamma-photon directions before a scintillator and photodetectors measure events. Rotating projections reconstruct the tracer distribution.

  6. 6. Acquisition computer

    Firmware or a digital processor applies calibration, validates signal quality, computes the useful quantity and supervises the system state.

  7. 7. Reconstruction

    A mathematical reconstruction combines spatially encoded measurements or multiple projections into an image. Acquisition quality limits the result.

Supervision: Detector-channel fault invalidates the affected signal chain. The processor must check signal quality before presenting a result or enabling an output.

◈Real-World Applications

✦

Brain perfusion

Rotating gamma projections reconstruct a regional tracer-distribution map.

◉

Myocardial perfusion

Gamma-camera measurements support comparison of myocardial tracer distribution.

◇

Bone scanning

Tracer localization can reveal patterns of activity in bone.

✦

Renal function imaging

Timed gamma measurements support investigation of tracer transport and renal function.
