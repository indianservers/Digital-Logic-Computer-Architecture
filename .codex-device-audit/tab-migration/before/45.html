Build This Device — System Architecture
  1. 1. Sensing leads

    Conductive contacts acquire a differential biopotential or provide the supervised output interface. Contact impedance affects signal quality.

  2. 2. Input amplifier

    A high-input-impedance front end amplifies the useful signal while reducing common-mode interference. Gain and headroom determine the sampling range.

  3. 3. ADC

    An analog-to-digital converter quantizes sampled voltage into binary codes. Reference voltage, resolution and sample timing affect the result.

  4. 4. Rhythm classifier

    The detector evaluates rate and rhythm characteristics before a supervised therapy decision; noise must not be mistaken for an arrhythmia.

  5. 5. Microcontroller

    Firmware or a digital processor applies calibration, validates signal quality, computes the useful quantity and supervises the system state.

  6. 6. Battery

    The power source feeds regulated rails. Low supply voltage can reduce measurement reliability and must be detected by supervision.

  7. 7. Energy store

    Stored energy and output readiness are supervised independently from the low-power rhythm sensing chain.

  8. 8. Telemetry

    Telemetry connects to the acquisition or control chain. An ICD continuously evaluates rhythm and may coordinate programmed pacing or defibrillation. Detection must distinguish rapid rhythms from interference and inappropriate triggers.

Supervision: Lead sensing fault invalidates the affected signal chain. The processor must check signal quality before presenting a result or enabling an output.
