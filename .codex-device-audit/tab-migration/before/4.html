◈Device Overview

Track pressure oscillations in an inflatable cuff to estimate systolic and diastolic blood pressure.

Key principle

A cuff pressure sensor detects small pulse-related oscillations during deflation. Their amplitude envelope supports an oscillometric estimate; results depend on validated algorithms and cuff fit.

Build This Device — System Architecture
  1. 1. Inflatable cuff

    The pneumatic stage changes cuff pressure. Slow deflation exposes pulse-related oscillations whose envelope supports an oscillometric estimate.

  2. 2. Air pump

    The pneumatic stage changes cuff pressure. Slow deflation exposes pulse-related oscillations whose envelope supports an oscillometric estimate.

  3. 3. Pressure sensor

    A transducer converts pressure or force into an electrical signal. Conditioning and calibration produce a supervised mechanical measurement.

  4. 4. Deflation valve

    The pneumatic stage changes cuff pressure. Slow deflation exposes pulse-related oscillations whose envelope supports an oscillometric estimate.

  5. 5. Analog front end

    A high-input-impedance front end amplifies the useful signal while reducing common-mode interference. Gain and headroom determine the sampling range.

  6. 6. ADC

    An analog-to-digital converter quantizes sampled voltage into binary codes. Reference voltage, resolution and sample timing affect the result.

  7. 7. MCU

    Firmware or a digital processor applies calibration, validates signal quality, computes the useful quantity and supervises the system state.

  8. 8. LCD

    The interface presents the processed quantity or stores acquired data with its timing and validity state.

Supervision: Cuff leak invalidates the affected signal chain. The processor must check signal quality before presenting a result or enabling an output.
