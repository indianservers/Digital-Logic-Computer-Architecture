Lab 96 — Sonar Detection System

Sound for a Safer, Deeper World

Active sonar emits a pulse through a projector and receives target echoes on a hydrophone array. A low-noise amplifier, filter and ADC supply DSP beamforming and detection.

◈ Working Principle (Step by Step)

  1. The projector emits a virtual acoustic pulse.

  2. Sound travels through water.

  3. Targets return a portion of the pulse.

  4. Hydrophones convert sound pressure into voltage.

  5. Condition the weak echo.

  6. ADC samples preserve the receive waveform.

  7. DSP estimates target delay and bearing.

  8. Show synthetic targets and their quality.

◈ Key Takeaways

✓ Sonar detects underwater objects with sound

✓ Projector and hydrophones transmit and receive

✓ LNA, filter and ADC prepare weak echoes

✓ DSP and beamforming estimate delay and bearing

✓ Active range uses half the round-trip distance

✓ Passive listening supplies bearings without direct range

✓ All acoustic signals are synthetic.

✓ Passive mode has no direct pulse range.

✓ Sound speed varies with ocean conditions.
