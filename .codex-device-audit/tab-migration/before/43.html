Build This Device — System Architecture
  1. 1. Pads

    Conductive contacts acquire a differential biopotential or provide the supervised output interface. Contact impedance affects signal quality.

  2. 2. ECG front end

    A high-input-impedance front end amplifies the useful signal while reducing common-mode interference. Gain and headroom determine the sampling range.

  3. 3. Rhythm analysis

    Rhythm analysis connects to the acquisition or control chain. An AED analyzes an acquired ECG and only advises a shock for a shockable rhythm when acquisition and safety checks permit it. Non-shockable rhythms require a different response.

  4. 4. Safety controller

    Firmware or a digital processor applies calibration, validates signal quality, computes the useful quantity and supervises the system state.

  5. 5. Battery

    The power source feeds regulated rails. Low supply voltage can reduce measurement reliability and must be detected by supervision.

  6. 6. Energy storage

    The interface presents the processed quantity or stores acquired data with its timing and validity state.

  7. 7. Audio prompts

    Audio prompts connects to the acquisition or control chain. An AED analyzes an acquired ECG and only advises a shock for a shockable rhythm when acquisition and safety checks permit it. Non-shockable rhythms require a different response.

  8. 8. Output supervisor

    Output supervisor connects to the acquisition or control chain. An AED analyzes an acquired ECG and only advises a shock for a shockable rhythm when acquisition and safety checks permit it. Non-shockable rhythms require a different response.

Supervision: Poor pad contact invalidates the affected signal chain. The processor must check signal quality before presenting a result or enabling an output.
